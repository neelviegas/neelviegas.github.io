Post-Disaster Situational Mapping (MECH 390)

Tools: SolidWorks · Topology Optimization · FEA · Drawings & BOM · DFA/DFM Timeline: Sep 2026 – Nov 2026

Goal (assigned): Design a fleet of drones that map a damaged area after a disaster. Mine is the thermal-camera drone, used to find people and hot spots under debris and smoke (the other drones carry LIDAR and ground-penetrating radar).

I designed a medium-sized drone that carries a protected thermal camera, and built the full assembly in SolidWorks: arms, base plates, bought motors, props, flight controller, battery and mount. Apart from the bought parts and standard hardware, I designed every part myself (the base plates and arms are modified from the class frame).

The camera sits in a servo-driven U bracket that tilts from facing forward to straight down, turning on a ball bearing. The camera module hangs from rubber ball dampers to reduce the effect of propeller vibration on the images, and the camera lives inside a PC/ABS housing that protects it from debris. To reduce mass, I did a topology study with 20 N per motor, which showed max bending stress was around 24 MPa against a 276 MPa yield. With the results, I redesigned the arms and cut arm mass from 102.4 g to 85.5 g each (68 g over 4 arms).

Result: My part of the project (this drone) received a grade of 100% (and became a poster on my wall)

Thrust Analysis Tool (McGill Rocket Team)

Tools: Python · Matplotlib · NumPy Timeline: May 2026 – Sep 2026

Goal: A python pipeline that takes in hotfire test data and outputs the coefficient of combustion efficiency.

It takes in raw Hotfire data from our hybrid rocket engine and spits out the combustion efficiency (and some pretty graphs). The main difficulties revolved around making assumptions to deal with faulty sensors and deciding if I could trust the data. I used Python Matplotlib and NumPy to get to the final product.

Results: c* efficiency avg = 78.2%

Automatic Curtains (Personal Project)

Tools: SolidWorks · Arduino UNO · 3D print Timeline: Jun 2026 – Jul 2026

Goal: I live next to a building whose lights, unfortunately for me, never turn off. That said, I like waking up to the sun without compromising my sleep. The goal was therefore to build curtains that open on a timer.

I designed a custom gear train and servo mount in SolidWorks, then 3D printed them. I built the circuit around an Arduino UNO with a timed open/close routine. I iterated on the gear set until the torque and fit were right, then drilled the permanent solution into my wall.

Result: Fully assembled product & better sleep

Vortex Injector Redesign (McGill Rocket Team)

Tools: Siemens NX · GD&T Timeline: Aug 2025 – May 2026

Goal: Update all versions of McGill Rocket Team's vortex injectors to reflect the most recent research.

New research showed that a ~45° hole angle resulted in better combustion stability and regression. I presented and justified my research to the team, then redesigned both injectors in Siemens NX and produced new manufacturing drawings, including the full GD&T, sections, and detail views needed to machine the parts.

Result: Authored new drawings with tolerances, seal callouts, and detail scales
